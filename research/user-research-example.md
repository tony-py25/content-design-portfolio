# User Research Example

## Project: AfriLang AI

**Role:** Content Designer / Researcher

**Research type:** Exploratory user research

---

## Research Objective

The objective was to understand the different information needs of people who might use an African-language AI data marketplace.

The research focused on two primary audiences:

- Language contributors
- AI companies and researchers

The goal was to identify potential questions, concerns, and barriers that content should address.

---

# Research Questions

### Contributors

I would investigate questions such as:

- What motivates people to contribute language data?
- What concerns might they have about participating?
- What information would they need before contributing?
- Which parts of the process could be confusing?
- What terminology would be easiest for them to understand?

### Dataset Buyers

I would investigate:

- What information do buyers need before evaluating a dataset?
- Which dataset specifications are most important?
- What quality information do they expect?
- What questions do they have about licensing and permitted use?
- What would make them trust a new marketplace?

---

# Research Methods

For an early-stage product, I would combine several research methods.

### Interviews

Conduct short, structured interviews with potential contributors and potential dataset users.

### Surveys

Use surveys to identify common questions, expectations, and concerns across a larger group.

### Competitive Research

Review existing data marketplaces and AI-data platforms to identify common patterns and content gaps.

### Content Analysis

Analyze existing platform content to identify unclear terminology, missing information, and opportunities to simplify communication.

---

# Example User Insights

The following are **hypothetical research insights** that would need to be validated with real users.

### Insight 1

Potential contributors may not understand what "AI training data" means.

**Content response:**

Explain the concept in simple language before introducing technical terminology.

---

### Insight 2

Contributors may want to know how their language recordings will be used.

**Content response:**

Provide a clear explanation of data use, consent, and relevant terms before submission.

---

### Insight 3

Dataset buyers may need more information than simply the name of a dataset.

**Content response:**

Dataset pages should clearly explain language, data type, intended use, quality information, and licensing/request options.

---

### Insight 4

Users may have different levels of technical knowledge.

**Content response:**

Provide simple explanations first, with more detailed technical information available when needed.

---

# From Research to Content Decisions

Research findings should directly influence content decisions.

| User Need | Content Design Response |
|---|---|
| Understand the platform | Clear homepage introduction |
| Understand contribution | Step-by-step contributor guide |
| Understand datasets | Structured dataset descriptions |
| Understand technical terms | Plain-language explanations |
| Understand data use | Clear consent and data-use information |
| Find relevant datasets | Search and filtering structure |
| Know what happens next | Clear calls to action and confirmation messages |

# Example Interview Scenario

### User statement

> "I want to contribute my language, but I don't understand what happens to my recording after I submit it."

### Content Design Response

Instead of simply providing a technical privacy policy, the interface could provide a short explanation:

**What happens after you submit?**

Your language sample is reviewed for quality before it is accepted. Information about how the data may be used is provided before you submit your contribution.

**Button:** Learn how your data is used

The detailed policy can then provide additional information for users who want to learn more.

# Research Ethics

Research involving people should be conducted respectfully and transparently.

Important considerations include:

- Explain the purpose of the research
- Obtain appropriate consent
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information
- Protect participant information
- Allow participants to ask questions
- Represent findings accurately
- Avoid presenting assumptions as confirmed findings

---

# Research Limitations

Because AfriLang AI is an early-stage project, these examples represent a **research framework and hypothetical insights**, rather than completed user research findings.

The next stage would be to conduct research with actual potential contributors and dataset users.

Their feedback would be used to validate or change the assumptions presented here.

---

# How Research Influences My Content Design

I believe good content design begins with understanding the people who will use the product.

Research helps answer:

**Who are we designing for?**

**What are they trying to accomplish?**

**What information do they need?**

**Where might they become confused?**

**What language will help them move forward?**

The answers to these questions inform the structure, wording, and organization of content.
