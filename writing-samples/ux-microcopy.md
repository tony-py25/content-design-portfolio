UX Microcopy Writing Sample

Overview

Microcopy is the short text that helps users understand what to do within a digital product or service.

Good microcopy should be clear, concise, useful, and written around the user's immediate need.

This sample demonstrates how I approach common UX writing situations.

---

1. Form Instructions

Before

«Please ensure that the information provided in the fields below is accurate and complete before proceeding.»

Improved

Enter your details

Provide accurate information in all required fields.

---

2. Call-to-Action Button

Before

Submit

Improved

Continue to application

The improved button gives the user a clearer indication of what will happen next.

---

3. Missing Information

Before

«Error: Invalid submission.»

Improved

Some information is missing

Check the highlighted fields and complete them before continuing.

---

4. Successful Submission

Before

«Your request has been processed successfully.»

Improved

Application submitted

Your application has been received. We’ll provide an update when the next step is ready.

---

5. Password Requirement

Before

«Password does not meet the required criteria.»

Improved

Create a stronger password

Use at least 8 characters, including a combination of letters and numbers.

---

6. Empty Search Results

Before

«No results found.»

Improved

We couldn't find anything

Try a different search term or check your spelling.

---

7. Confirmation Before an Important Action

Before

«Are you sure you want to continue?»

Improved

Ready to continue?

Review your information before proceeding.

My UX Writing Principles

Be Clear

Users should understand the message without having to interpret technical language.

Be Concise

Use only the words necessary to communicate the message.

Be Helpful

When something goes wrong, explain what happened and, where possible, tell the user how to fix it.

Be Action-Oriented

Calls to action should clearly communicate what will happen when the user selects them.

Be Consistent

Similar actions and messages should use consistent terminology throughout a digital experience.
