AfriLang AI — African Language AI Data Marketplace

Project Overview

AfriLang AI is a proposed African-language AI data marketplace designed to connect African-language contributors with organizations developing AI systems.

The project starts with Kiswahili and is designed to support the creation of high-quality language datasets for applications such as speech recognition, translation, conversational AI, voice technology, and AI evaluation.

The Problem

Many AI systems require large amounts of high-quality language data. However, African languages and regional language varieties can be underrepresented in AI datasets.

This creates challenges for organizations developing AI products that need reliable African-language data.

AfriLang AI addresses this opportunity by creating a structured marketplace for collecting, organizing, quality-checking, and providing African-language datasets.

Content Challenge

The concept involves several audiences with different needs:

- AI companies looking for language datasets
- African-language contributors providing language data
- Researchers and developers working with African languages
- Organizations interested in AI evaluation and localization

The content therefore needs to explain a technically complex marketplace in language that different audiences can understand quickly.

Content Strategy

I structured the project around three core questions:

1. What problem does AfriLang AI solve?

It helps address the shortage of accessible, high-quality African-language data for AI development.

2. Who is it for?

The platform connects language-data contributors with AI developers, researchers, and organizations that need African-language datasets.

3. What does the platform provide?

Potential datasets and services include:

- Voice recordings
- Transcriptions
- Translations
- Text datasets
- Conversation datasets
- Human evaluation of AI responses
- Regional and dialect language data

Content Design Principles

Plain Language

Technical concepts are explained in straightforward language so that both technical and non-technical users can understand the service.

User-Centred Structure

Information is organized according to the needs of contributors and buyers rather than the internal structure of the organization.

Clear Calls to Action

Users should immediately understand whether they should explore datasets, become a contributor, or request data.

Scannability

Headings, short sections, bullet points, and concise descriptions make the platform easier to navigate.

Trust and Transparency

Dataset descriptions should clearly communicate what the data contains, how it was collected, quality considerations, and applicable licensing or consent requirements.
